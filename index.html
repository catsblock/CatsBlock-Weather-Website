How to use

Up and running in one minute.

Install

Download the APK from the Download page, open it and allow your browser to install apps if Android asks.

Allow location

Tap Allow for your current weather, or skip it and search for any city instead.

Pick a source

Open Settings → Weather data and choose Open-Meteo, WeatherAPI.com, Visual Crossing or OpenWeather.

Turn on alerts

Open Alerts & warnings, switch on what you want and choose the time. Add a widget from your home screen.

Good to know

Tap and hold a weather card to play its effect, swipe between cities, open any detail card for charts, and use the Travel screen for a trip forecast. If alerts do not arrive, set the app’s battery usage to “Unrestricted” and keep notifications allowed.

Security

“Play Protect warned me.” Here’s why.

It is normal to see a warning when you install CatsBlock Weather. It does not mean the app is harmful — it means Google has not seen this app before.

Why the warning appears

Play Protect is cautious with apps installed outside Google Play. In practice, the warning appears because:

  • The APK does not come from Google Play and is signed by an independent developer.
  • Each new build is a brand-new file that Google has never scanned.
  • The app has features Play Protect treats carefully: install permission for its built-in updater, location, notifications, camera and background sync.

Why it stops after install

Play Protect’s unfamiliar-app check happens when you install. Tap More details → Install anyway (or Scan app) and the file is analyzed. When nothing harmful is found, it does not warn again while you use the app. A later update can show the prompt once more, because it is a new file.

You may see a Play Protect warning

It says Google has not seen this app before. That is expected for apps installed outside Google Play, and it is not a malware finding. Use More details → Install anyway or Scan app. Do not turn Play Protect off.

Why Play Protect detects it

Play Protect checks apps for harmful behavior and also treats unfamiliar apps carefully. In practice, a few things make CatsBlock Weather look “new” to it:

Installed outside Google PlayThe APK comes from GitHub, not the Play Store, and is signed with the developer’s own key rather than a key Google manages.
Every build is a new fileEach release is a new APK with a new fingerprint. Google has not scanned it yet, so the first install of each build can show a prompt.
Sensitive-sounding permissionsThe built-in updater needs permission to install packages, plus location, notifications, camera (Live Moon) and background sync. Each one is explained below.

Why it doesn’t warn after installing

The unfamiliar-app check runs at install time. When you choose Scan app or Install anyway, the app is analyzed. If no harmful behavior is found, Play Protect has nothing to flag while you use it, so the warning does not come back. When you update to a new build, you might see the prompt once more — that is the same new-file effect.

Is it safe? Check it yourself

You should not have to take anyone’s word for it. These are things you can verify:

The source code is publicRead it at github.com/catsblock/CatsBlock-Weather-v2.
The APK is built in publicReleases are produced by a GitHub Actions workflow in that repository (Build APK) and published as build-N releases, so you can see exactly how each file was made.
Compare the SHA-256When GitHub provides it, the checksum is shown on this page. Compare it with the file you downloaded (sha256sum file.apk on a computer, or any hash app on Android).
Scan it with VirusTotalUpload the APK to virustotal.com and read the results from many engines. A single odd result from one engine on a new APK is common; look at the overall picture.
Review the permissionsAndroid shows them before you install and in Settings → Apps → CatsBlock Weather → Permissions. The app works with location and camera denied.
No ads, accounts or analyticsThe privacy policy states the app has none, and stores your cities and preferences only on your device.

If anything doesn’t match, don’t install. Download only from this page or the repository’s Releases.

Privacy Policy

How CatsBlock Weather handles your data. Last updated 2026-10-27.

Company and app

This Privacy Policy applies to the application CatsBlock Weather (the “App”), published by CatsBlock Corporation (“CatsBlock Corporation”, “we”, “us”).

By downloading, opening or using CatsBlock Weather, you acknowledge that you have read this Privacy Policy and agree to it. If you do not agree, do not use the App.

Data we process

CatsBlock Corporation does not run user accounts, analytics or advertising, does not sell your data and does not build user profiles. The App stores saved cities and preferences on your device. When a Cloudflare Worker data source is used, limited technical data is processed to operate and protect the service: your IP address and approximate coordinates, as described below.

Location permission

If you allow location access, your position is read on your device to find the weather where you are. When a data source that uses the Cloudflare Worker is selected, approximate coordinates (rounded to about 1 km) are sent through it. You can revoke location permission at any time in your device settings.

Data stored on your device

Saved cities and your preferences are stored only on your device and can be removed at any time by clearing the App data or uninstalling the App.

Visual effects and touch input

Rain, snow and touch effects are drawn on your device using its graphics processor. Where you touch a weather card is used only to animate the effect on screen; touch positions are never stored, logged or sent anywhere.

Third-party public APIs

To show weather, coordinates and city searches are sent to free, public third-party services such as Open-Meteo, WeatherAPI.com, Visual Crossing and OpenWeather. Moon images and map tiles are loaded from public providers such as NASA and OpenStreetMap. These providers receive your IP address as part of the connection, are not operated by CatsBlock Corporation, and are governed by their own terms and privacy policies.

When you use the travel planner, the places you type are sent to Open-Meteo to find their coordinates, the start and destination coordinates are sent to the public OSRM routing service to calculate the route, and coordinates of points along the route are sent to Open-Meteo to get the forecast. These services are not operated by CatsBlock Corporation. The planner does not store routes on any CatsBlock Corporation server.

Cloudflare Worker proxy

When the WeatherAPI.com, Visual Crossing or OpenWeather data source is selected, requests go through a Cloudflare Worker operated by CatsBlock Corporation, which keeps API keys off your device and limits abuse. To enforce request limits it uses only your IP address; the Worker keeps only an irreversible hash of it, for at most 12 hours, and does not store the original value. No device identifier is collected or sent. Weather responses for rounded coordinates are cached for about 15 minutes. Cloudflare, as infrastructure provider, may process connection data under its own terms. The Open-Meteo data source does not use this proxy.
